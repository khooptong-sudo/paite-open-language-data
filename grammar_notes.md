# Paite Grammar Notes

Owner-curated notes from live conversation with Levia. These are working hypotheses, not a formal grammar.

## Pronouns

| Person | Subject | Object |
|--------|---------|--------|
| I | Ka | hon (?) |
| you | Na / Nang | Na hon / N'on |

- `Na hon` / `N'on` = "you" (object form).
- `hon` may be a general object marker / first-person object pronoun (= me).

## Verbs

| English | Paite |
|---------|-------|
| know | thei |
| love | it |
| see / look / check | en |
| do | chi / chim / chih ? |

- `didan` appears in "What are you doing?" — possibly an auxiliary or verb stem for "do".
- `chi` / `chim` seem related to "matter / thing / do".

## Particles / question markers

- `hia?` / `hiam?` = question marker (yes/no or open question).
- `e` = emphatic/assertive particle? (`Ka dam e.` = "I am well.")
- `le?` = question particle? (`Hiai te bang chih didan a le?`)
- `a` = verb/assertive particle? Common in biblical text.
- `te` = plural marker? (`Hiai te` = "these")

## Numbers and counting

The numeral follows the noun. The noun does not change for plural: `suang` (rock) stays the same with every number.

| Paite | English |
|-------|---------|
| Suang | rock / rocks |
| Suang khat | 1 rock |
| Suang nih | 2 rocks |
| Suang thum | 3 rocks |
| Suang nga | 5 rocks |
| Suang sawm | 10 rocks |
| Suang za / Suang zakhat | 100 rocks (`za` = hundred, `zakhat` = one hundred) |
| Suang sangkhat | 1,000 rocks (`sang` = thousand) |

Zero is not said with a number. It is said with a negative sentence:

| Paite | English |
|-------|---------|
| Leilak ah suang ka mukei. | I don't see any rocks on the ground. (`leilak ah` = on the ground) |
| Suang khat le ka mukei. | I don't see even one rock. |
| Suang khat leilak ah ka mu. | I see 1 rock on the ground. |
| Suang sawm leilak ah ka mu. | I see 10 rocks on the ground. |
| Suang leilak ah ka mu. | I see rocks on the ground. |

- `kei` after the verb (`mu` = see) marks the negative. (`ka mu` = I see, `ka mukei` = I don't see). Confirmed by the owner.
- `le` after a quantity means "even". (`suang khat le` = even one rock). Confirmed by the owner.
- Word order in these sentences: object (with its number), then place (`leilak ah`), then subject marker (`ka`), then verb (`mu`).

## Modern Paite and Tedim (Bible) style

Older Bible text and AI models often write in the Tedim (Tedim-Chin) style. Modern Paite, as spoken and written in Manipur, Mizoram, and Bangladesh, uses different forms. Both are understood, but they are not the same.

| Meaning | Modern Paite | Tedim / Bible style |
|---------|--------------|---------------------|
| Do you love me? | Na hon it hia? | Nang in kei nong it hiam? |
| I don't see even one rock. | Suang khat le ka mukei. | Suang khat zong ka mu kei hi. |

- Tedim / Bible style markers: `nong`, `hiam` (question), `zong` (even), `hi` at the end of a statement.
- Modern Paite uses `hia` (question) and `le` (even), and often leaves out the final `hi`.
- `Suang khat lei-ah ka mu hi.` (I see one rock on the ground) is acceptable Paite, though `Suang khat leilak ah ka mu.` is the more usual form.

## Words with more than one sense

- `mangpha` = good night, sweet dreams. `Mangpha phot le.` = Good night for now.
- `mang` = dream; also lost.
- `pha` = good, in set greetings: `mangpha` (good night), `zingpha` (good morning). It is not the everyday word for "good" in other uses.
